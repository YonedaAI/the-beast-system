## 1. Evaluation by Criterion

### 1.1 Mathematical Correctness
The core formal results—Proposition 2.5 (lines 149–171), Corollary 2.6 (lines 175–182), Lemma 3.5 (lines 293–300), Proposition 3.7 (lines 342–354), Proposition 5.2 (lines 546–559), Proposition 5.4 (lines 574–586), and Proposition 5.5 (lines 601–617)—have valid proofs under their stated or intended assumptions, and the involution argument for veto players in Proposition 2.5(e) (line 170) is clean. However, five formal flaws require correction:

1. **Missing hypothesis in Lemma 3.4 and Theorem 3.6(a) (lines 284–291, 309–316, 324):** Definition 2.2 (lines 102–105) requires that collective decisions be selected by "some set of members or practices of the collective," and Section 3.2 (line 324) explicitly states that a collective decision imposed by an outside authority or directly by the environment "is excluded by the self-government clause." Yet the self-government clause resides in Assumption 3.3 (lines 277–280), while Lemma 3.4 (line 286) and the first sentence of Theorem 3.6 (line 311) invoke only Assumption 3.1 (Plurality). Without Assumption 3.3, Assumption 3.1 neither guarantees that the group persists long enough to realize decisions nor excludes decisions imposed from outside.
2. **Ill-defined quotient game for bloc shares $s^{\pP}(S)$ (line 594):** Section 5.3 defines the bloc share $s^{\pP}(S)$ as the Shapley-Shubik share of $S$ in the game obtained "by replacing the members of $S$ with a single player who belongs to a coalition exactly when all of $S$ does." In a simple game $(N,\Win)$, a coalition is an arbitrary subset of players; what must be defined is which subsets of the reduced player set $N_S = (N\setminus S)\cup\{p_S\}$ are *winning* ($\Win_S$). Specifically, if a winning coalition $C\in\Win$ intersects $S$ properly ($\emptyset \neq C\cap S \subsetneq S$, as with $\{M,G\}$ when $S=\{G,E\}$ in the Iranian example of Table 2, line 204), mapping $C$ by asking whether $S\subseteq C$ would map $\{M,G\}$ to $\{M\}$, falsely making $M$ a dictator. The intended quotient game $\Win_S$ on $N_S$ is defined for $T\subseteq N\setminus S$ by $T\in\Win_S \iff T\in\Win$ and $T\cup\{p_S\}\in\Win_S \iff T\cup S\in\Win$.
3. **Unindexed office compliance $f_o$ in Proposition 5.5 (lines 603–607):** Proposition 5.5 defines $f_o\in[0,1]$ as "the share of the office holder's decisions that follow the directives of the coalition that appointed the holder or can remove the holder." Because $\mathcal{S}$ is a family of coalitions and Definition 2.8 (line 229) triggers when $S$ can unilaterally appoint *or* remove the holder of $o$, two distinct coalitions $S_1, S_2\in\mathcal{S}$ can satisfy the indicator for the same office $o$ (for instance, when $S_1$ appoints and $S_2$ can unilaterally remove, or when $S_1\subset S_2$). Compliance with the directives of $S$ must therefore be indexed by both coalition and office ($f_{S,o}$).
4. **Conflation of veto players with winning coalitions in Proposition 5.4(c) (lines 569–586):** Definition 2.4 (line 144) defines a veto player $i$ by $i\in\bigcap_{S\in\Win} S$. Non-veto players may still be required to assemble a winning coalition (e.g., one veto player plus a majority of non-veto legislators). Definition 5.3 (line 571) defines the winset $W_K(q)$ using only a set $K$ of veto players, and Proposition 5.4(c) (line 580) states that "$x$ can replace $q$ if and only if $K\cap B=\emptyset$," while the proof (line 585) equates "$x$ can replace $q$" with $x\in W_K(q)$. In a general simple game, $x\in W_K(q)$ is necessary for $x$ to replace $q$, but it is sufficient only when $K$ is itself a winning coalition ($\Win=\{S: K\subseteq S\}$).
5. **Existential ambiguity in Proposition 3.7(c) (lines 348, 353):** Proposition 3.7(c) states that if a group may split into subgroups $N_1,\dots,N_r$, "then a change of rules for some members is possible even when $q$ is Pareto optimal for the whole group." Under Definition 2.1 (line 99), a collective requires $|N_1|\ge 2$. If $q$ is ranked first by $n_a-1$ adults and last by a single dissenter, no subgroup of size $\ge 2$ has a nonempty consensus winset. The proposition should state the exact condition under which fission enables rule change: $W_{N_1}(q)\neq\emptyset$ for some subgroup $N_1\subset N_a$ with $|N_1|\ge 2$ if and only if at least two adults rank the same alternative $x$ above $q$.

### 1.2 Clarity
The conceptual distinction between rule making ($\pP$) and adjudication ($\pJ$) by act rather than organization (Definition 2.3, line 109; Table 1, lines 116–136) is clear and well motivated. However, three conceptual ambiguities weaken the paper's clarity:

1. **Level-of-analysis shifting in the player set $N$ (lines 86, 99, 188, 344, 448, 482):** Section 2.1 (line 86) defines nodes $V_t$ as "organizations or coherent actor groups." Section 2.2 (line 99) and Proposition 3.7 (line 344) define $N$ as the set of individual members or adults in a collective ($H^{\pP}=1/n_a$). Section 2.4 (line 188, Table 2) and Section 4.4 (line 448) define $N$ as unitary parties, chambers, or peak associations ($H^{\pP}=1/3$ or $1/2$). Section 4.7 (line 482) then states that under referendums "the electorate itself becomes a veto player." Without a formal compound-game rule (Shapley 1964; Owen 1977) or a fixed level of aggregation, $H^{\pP}$ across individual-level bands and macro-institutional states is not on the same scale.
2. **Three inconsistent definitions of "capture" (lines 86–91, 535, 682):** Section 2.1 defines a dyadic *capture link* $(i\to j, m)$ (privileged access, weakened accountability, and material evidence of an institutional output) and a system-level *strong classification* (high $U_t$ and high $\Int_t$). Yet Section 7 (line 682) refers to "contestability near zero and concentration near one, the joint condition that defines capture in a single pillar" (i.e., $H^{\pP}(1-Q^{\pP})\approx 1$), which appears nowhere in Section 2.
3. **Construct mismatch between de jure and de facto control shares (Table 5, lines 647–648; Section 8, line 697):** De jure shares are defined via Shapley-Shubik pivotality over enactment (veto and voting power), whereas de facto shares in Table 5 (line 648) are defined as "Shares of enacted rules by originating actor, from legislative records and decree registers." Because initiation and veto power are orthogonal institutional roles—for example, in the United States the president originates zero statutes directly yet holds a veto, while in bicameral systems one chamber may originate all revenue bills—comparing the two indicators confounds agenda initiation with de facto enactment power.

### 1.3 Completeness
Within its qualitative scope, the literature review (Section 4) and six-regime comparison (Section 5) are learned and balanced. The main completeness deficit is **external dependency on unpublished companion papers**: the manuscript relies on seven working papers from October 2026 (`largo2026hbs` and `long2026a`–`long2026f`), including Part VII (`long2026formalized`, listed as "in preparation" on line 890), to supply the formal definitions of capture (line 93), the general axioms of Proposition A and Proposition B (line 69), and the formal definition of cross-pillar integration $C_{mn}$ (line 594). A standalone journal article must state the self-contained formal definitions of all objects on which its propositions and hypotheses rely.

The progression from network primitives (Section 2) to necessity (Section 3), literature (Section 4), comparative analysis (Section 5), formal mechanisms (Section 6), and measurement/hypotheses (Section 7) is generally logical, with three structural flaws:

Because nothing in the analytical sections depends on Section 7 (as noted on line 701), it belongs either in an appendix or after Section 8.
2. **Placement of Literature Review (Section 4, lines 374–412) after the Formal Models of Sections 2–3:** Standard analytical structure places the literature gap and motivation (Section 4.6, lines 409–412) before the formal framework that resolves those gaps, or integrates the literature into the mechanisms (Section 5) where selectorate theory, Michels's law, Tsebelis's veto players, and Culpepper's quiet politics are modeled a second time.
3. **Misplaced notation remark (Remark 2.9, lines 245–248):** Remark 2.9 explains why $\ell$ replaces $n$ as the pillar index, but $\ell$ is already used in Definition 2.6 (line 227) with a parenthetical forward reference to Remark 2.9.

> Religious jurists in Islamic polities can either raise the oversight component of contestability ($q^{\mathrm{ovs}}$) by checking the ruler or lower the entry component ($q^{\mathrm{ent}}$) by excluding rival lawmakers, depending on their fiscal and institutional independence from the state. ## 3. Structured Feedback by Severity

### 3.1 Critical Issues
1. **Omission of self-government and persistence assumptions in Lemma 3.4 and Theorem 3.6(a) (lines 267–291, 309–316, 324):**
   - *Issue:* Lemma 3.4 (line 286) and the first sentence of Theorem 3.6 (line 311) state that a collective satisfying Assumption 3.1 (Plurality) alone implements the rule-making function $\pP$ in sense (a) of Definition 2.2 (line 104). However, Definition 2.2(a) requires that *members or practices of the collective* repeatedly select collective decisions over $T$, and Section 3.2 (line 324) explicitly relies on the self-government clause of Assumption 3.3 (line 279) to rule out external lotteries, colonial imposition, or environmental forcing.
   - *Fix:* Add Assumption 3.3 (Persistence and self-government) to the hypotheses of Lemma 3.4 and to the first statement of Theorem 3.6.
2. **Construct mismatch between de jure and de facto control shares (Table 5, lines 647–648; Section 8, line 697):**
   - *Issue:* De jure control shares $\varphi_i$ (Definition 2.4, line 144; Table 5, line 647) measure pivotal voting and veto power over enactment. By contrast, Table 5 (line 648) operationalizes de facto control shares as "Shares of enacted rules by originating actor, from legislative records and decree registers," and Section 8 (line 697) claims that the gap between the two reveals informal power. Because formal initiation and veto power are distinct—an actor with an absolute veto may originate no bills, and a single chamber may originate all bills that require multi-actor consent—the difference between bill origination share and Shapley-Shubik share does not measure de facto versus de jure power.
   - *Fix:* Replace or supplement the de facto indicator in Table 5 with an outcome-based measure of pivotal influence (such as success rates on contested amendments, roll-call pivotality, or survival of actor-sponsored provisions through markup and veto bargaining).
3. **Uncommensurable levels of actor aggregation in $H^{\pP}$ (lines 86, 99, 188, 201–205, 344–350, 448, 482):**
   - *Issue:* Concentration $H^{\pP}=\sum_i \varphi_i^2$ scales as $1/k$ for $k$ symmetric veto players (Proposition 2.5(d)). Because Section 3.3 (Proposition 3.7, line 346) defines players $N$ as individual adults ($H^{\pP}=1/n_a$), whereas Section 2.4 (Table 2, lines 201–205) and Section 4 (lines 448, 482) define players as aggregate chambers, parties, peak associations, or the entire electorate as a single actor, $H^{\pP}$ cannot be compared across stateless groups and state polities or even across parliamentary and corporatist arenas within the same state.
   - *Fix:* Explicitly specify a level-of-analysis convention (or introduce compound simple games where collective bodies aggregate individual members) and distinguish micro-level concentration (across individuals) from inter-organ concentration (across constitutional bodies).

### 3.2 Major Issues
1. **Imprecise definition of the bloc game for $s^{\pP}(S)$ (line 594):**
   - *Issue:* Defining the reduced game by "replacing the members of $S$ with a single player who belongs to a coalition exactly when all of $S$ does" does not specify what happens to winning coalitions $C\in\Win$ that contain a proper nonempty subset of $S$ (such as $\{M,G\}$ when $S=\{G,E\}$ in Table 2, line 204).
   - *Fix:* Define the quotient simple game $(N_S,\Win_S)$ on $N_S=(N\setminus S)\cup\{p_S\}$ explicitly: for any $T\subseteq N\setminus S$, $T\in\Win_S \iff T\in\Win$, and $T\cup\{p_S\}\in\Win_S \iff T\cup S\in\Win$.
2. **Missing coalition index on $f_o$ in Proposition 5.5 (lines 603–607):**
   - *Issue:* $f_o$ is defined as compliance with "the coalition that appointed the holder or can remove the holder," which is ambiguous whenever distinct coalitions $S_1, S_2\in\mathcal{S}$ can respectively appoint and remove the holder of $o$, or when one coalition in $\mathcal{S}$ is a subcoalition of another.
   - *Fix:* Replace $f_o$ with $f_{S,o}\in[0,1]$, denoting the share of decisions by the holder of $o$ that follow the directives of coalition $S$.
3. **Conflation of necessary and sufficient conditions in Proposition 5.4(c) (lines 571, 580, 585):**
   - *Issue:* In Proposition 5.4(c), $x\in W_K(q)$ is equated with "$x$ can replace $q$." If $K$ is a proper subset of the players needed to form a winning coalition (i.e., non-veto players exist), $x\in W_K(q)$ is necessary but not sufficient for replacement.
   - *Fix:* Either specify in Definition 5.3 / Proposition 5.4 that the procedure is the unanimity game $\Win=\{S: K\subseteq S\}$ on $K$, or change "can replace $q$" in Proposition 5.4(c) to "lies in the winset $W_K(q)$ (is unblocked by veto players)."
4. **Inconsistent definitions of capture across sections (lines 86–91, 535, 682):**
   - *Issue:* Section 2.1 defines capture links $(i\to j,m)$ and system-level integration $\Int_t$ alongside $U_t$, whereas line 682 defines "capture in a single pillar" as $Q^{\pP}\approx 0$ and $H^{\pP}\approx 1$.
   - *Fix:* Use distinct terms consistently: *unaccountable concentration* in pillar $\pP$ for $H^{\pP}(1-Q^{\pP})$, *dyadic capture link* for $(i\to j,\pP)$, and *systemic capture* for the joint condition on $U_t$ and $\Int_t$.
5. **Over-reliance on unpublished companion manuscripts (lines 69, 71, 86, 93, 219, 250, 254, 256, 594):**
   - *Issue:* Key definitions and background propositions are deferred to seven unpublished October 2026 working papers (`largo2026hbs` and `long2026a`–`long2026f`, with `long2026formalized` "in preparation").
   - *Fix:* State the self-contained mathematical definitions of all required objects (specifically the capture link conditions on line 91 and the formal definition of $C_{mn,t}$ on line 594) directly in Section 2.
6. **Structural placement of Section 7 (`Revelation 13 and the rule-making pillar`, lines 677–689):**
   - *Issue:* Placing the theological reading between Section 6 (`Measurement and hypotheses`) and Section 8 (`Objections and scope conditions`) disrupts the social-scientific sequence.
   - *Fix:* Move Section 7 after Section 8 (immediately before the Conclusion) or into a dedicated appendix.
7. **House-style violations in multi-claim and signposting paragraphs (lines 77, 360, 428, 448, 462, 464, 594, 703):**
   - *Issue:* As detailed in Sections 1.6 and 2 above, these paragraphs bundle multiple claims, rely on meta-commentary and roadmap signposting, or place qualifications ahead of findings.
   - *Fix:* Split multi-claim paragraphs (lines 448, 462, 464, 703) so each paragraph argues a single claim, eliminate roadmap and series-bookkeeping sentences (lines 77, 93, 327, 535, 594), and state findings before qualifications (lines 428, 464).
