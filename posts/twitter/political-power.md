1/5 Who makes the rules? Part II of the Beast System series separates two questions: is rule making necessary, and how concentrated is it? The answers turn out to be independent.

2/5 From an axiom of plurality (preferences diverge, yet some choices bind everyone), every persistent collective must implement a rule-making function. Not a particular institution. In stateless societies: camp discussion, lineage moots, fission.

3/5 The sharp result: for a group of n members and every k from 1 to n, some decision procedure satisfies the necessity lemma with concentration exactly 1/k. A group can need rule making and still run it as one voice or as n.

4/5 Three conditional routes from rule making to capture: monitoring incentives that vanish as organizations grow, veto structures that protect preferential status quo rules, and appointment channels that couple rule making to courts, security services and the economy.

5/5 The ideology changes. The institutional functions remain.
