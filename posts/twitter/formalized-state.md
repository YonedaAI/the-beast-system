1/6 The six-pillar framework identifies an invariant structure of human governance, supported by historical observation and given precise expression through mathematics. Part VII of the Beast System series defines the Formalized State and proves four theorems.

2/6 Representation: every governance system satisfying the axioms is equivalent to a Formalized State structure. Content, ideology and procedure are forgotten. Roles, control shares, concentration, contestability, integration and dependencies are kept.

3/6 Necessity: six axioms (scarcity, plurality, generality, coordination, defection, maintenance) force the six functions. Each axiom can fail in a persistent model. Interpretation needs legitimation or coercion, not both.

4/6 Minimality: drop or merge any pillar and you identify systems that differ in a preserved property, with explicit witness pairs. Uniqueness: the abstraction is the coarsest one preserving those properties, with exactly six dimensions.

5/6 Structure is universal. Capture is contingent. Structures meeting every axiom occupy all four corners of the concentration and integration square, so having the structure does not imply capture.

6/6 'Beast System' is a typological reading of Revelation 13, a name for a structure, never for any group or regime. The ideology changes. The institutional functions remain.
