### Summary Assessment

Part VII synthesizes the six pillar papers (Parts I to VI) and the five-layer multiplex framework of Largo (2026) into a unified mathematical theory of the Formalized State ($\mathcal{F}$). Relative to six refined axioms of collective life ($\mathrm{Ax}1^*$ to $\mathrm{Ax}6^*$), four incidence axioms ($\mathrm{X1}, \mathrm{X2}, \mathrm{X3}, \mathrm{X5}$), and a preserved property set $P$ (role map, control shares, Herfindahl concentrations $H_m$, contestability $Q_m$, unaccountable concentration $U$, coalition-overlap couplings $C^{\ov}_{mn}$, integration $\Integ$, capture predicate $\Capt$, and dependency hypergraph $\mathcal{D}$), the paper establishes four main results:
1. **Representation (Theorem 7.4):** Every persistent collective $G \in \mathcal{G}$ satisfying the refined and incidence axioms is $\cong_A$-equivalent to a Formalized State structure $F_G = \pi(G) \in \mathcal{F}$.
2. **Necessity (Theorem 4.3, Theorem 5.9):** The six functions and the baseline dependencies $\Dzero$ are forced by $\mathrm{Ax}1^*$ to $\mathrm{Ax}6^*$ (with consistency and axiom independence proved in Proposition 4.4), and strong connectivity and cascade completeness of the dependency hypergraph $\Dstar$ are forced by $\mathrm{X1}, \mathrm{X2}, \mathrm{X3}, \mathrm{X5}$ (with the role of each premise isolated in Proposition 5.10).
3. **Minimality (Proposition 7.5):** Every proper coarsening $(c,\lambda)$ that drops or merges pillars identifies two systems $G_1, G_2 \in \mathcal{G}_*$ with $P(G_1) \neq P(G_2)$.
4. **Uniqueness (Theorem 7.7, Theorem 7.8):** The abstraction $\Phi: \mathcal{G}_* \to \mathcal{F}_*$ is terminal in the category $\mathbf{Abs}_P$ of $P$-preserving abstractions, unique up to a unique isomorphism, and has dimension count $|K| = 6$ among coarsenings of the six problem classes.

In addition, the paper proves that structural necessity and interdependence do not imply capture (Theorem 6.6), characterizes closure and reversibility in a nonlinear cross-pillar conversion model (Theorem 8.5), and proves non-equivalence against formal encodings of seven canonical predecessors (Proposition 10.1, Theorem 10.2). Every proof in the paper is mathematically sound, and every witness pair checks out upon independent recomputation. Only minor revisions are required to tighten hypothesis alignment between two results, clarify actor-set support in one witness construction, and polish a few prose and bibliographic details.

* **Definitions 2.1 to 2.3 (Section 2.2):** Definition 2.1 defines the class $\mathcal{G}$ of persistent human collectives using only member sets $N_t$ ($|N_t|\ge 2$), a rule system $R_t$, compliance/recognition sufficient to carry out collective decisions, and a nonnegative sustaining stock on a filtered probability space $(\Omega,\mathcal{F},(\mathcal{F}_t),\Pr)$ with $\Pr(\Pi)>0$. * **Lemma 4.1 (Forcing, Section 4.1):**
  * *(a)* Defining $S_n$ as the event that at least $n$ instances are left unsolved and functioning survives through the disposition of the first $n$ unsolved instances, the conditional survival bound $\Pr(S_n \mid S_{n-1}) \le 1-\kappa$ yields $\Pr(S_n) \le (1-\kappa)^n$ by induction from $\Pr(S_0)=1$. Because $\Pi \cap \{N \ge n\} \subseteq S_n$, $\Pr(\Pi \cap \{N \ge n\}) \le (1-\kappa)^n$, and taking $n \to \infty$ gives $\Pr(\Pi \cap \{N = \infty\}) = 0$. Valid.
  * *(b)* On $\Pi$, $N < \infty$ almost surely by (a), so among the first $k$ instances the number of unsolved instances is bounded by $N$ (and equals $N$ for all large $k$). As $k \to \infty$, the number of solved instances $k - N \to \infty$ and the solved fraction $(k-N)/k \to 1$. Valid.
* **Lemma 4.2 (Pillar necessity, Section 4.2):** Combines the six pillar theorems on the common persistence event $\Pi$ with $\Pr(\Pi)>0$:

  * $\pP, \pJ, \pI, \pC, \pW$: Direct applications of Lemma 3.4 and Theorem 3.7 of Part II, Theorem 3.3 of Part III (Lemma 4.1 with hazard $\kappa$), Theorem 3.5 of Part IV (every window of length $L$ lies inside $\Pi$), Theorem 3.4 of Part V, and Lemma 3.3 of Part VI. Valid.

### Evaluation of Thematic Criteria

1. **Non-triviality of the representation theorem and honesty of preserved/forgotten properties (Section 7.1 to 7.2):** The representation theorem (Theorem 7.4) is non-trivial because $\mathcal{G}$ (Definition 2.1) is specified without reference to the six pillars, whereas membership in $\mathcal{F}$ (Definition 7.1) requires nonempty holder sets $V_m \neq \emptyset$ for all $m \in \Mset$ (F1) and a strongly connected, cascade-complete dependency hypergraph containing $\Dzero$ (F4). The paper demonstrates that Theorem 7.4(a) can fail on $\mathcal{G}$: the six persistent collectives in Proposition 4.4(b) violate (F1), and the persistent collective in Proposition 4.4(a) / Proposition 5.10(a) satisfies (F1) to (F3) but violates (F4) because $\pW$ has in-degree zero. Landing in $\mathcal{F}$ therefore requires the substantive derivations of Theorem 4.3 and Theorem 5.9. Lemma 7.3 and Section 12 candidly state what $\cong_A$ preserves and what it forgets (rule content, belief content, procedural detail, actor identities/histories, aggregate capacity levels $x_t$, welfare outcomes, and moral evaluation).
2. **Scoping of the uniqueness theorem and correctness of the category-theoretic argument (Section 7.4):** Definition 7.6 defines $\mathbf{Abs}_P$ with objects given by surjective $P$-preserving maps $\alpha: \mathcal{G}_* \to S_\alpha$ and morphisms $\alpha \to \beta$ given by maps $h: S_\alpha \to S_\beta$ satisfying $h \circ \alpha = \beta$. The direction of morphisms runs from finer partitions to coarser partitions. Because every object $\alpha$ is surjective, hom-sets have size at most one (making $\mathbf{Abs}_P$ a thin/posetal category). Because $\alpha$ is $P$-preserving and $\Phi(G) = P(G)$, $h(\alpha(G)) = \Phi(G)$ defines the unique morphism $\alpha \to \Phi$ for every $\alpha \in \mathbf{Abs}_P$. Thus $\Phi$ is the **terminal object** in $\mathbf{Abs}_P$ (the coarsest $P$-preserving abstraction), unique up to a unique isomorphism. Remark 7.9 and Section 12 properly scope both Theorem 7.7 and Theorem 7.8: uniqueness is relative to $P$, and the dimension count $|K|=6$ in Theorem 7.8 applies to coarsenings of the six axiom-derived problem classes, while acknowledging that splitting a class (e.g., coercion into policing and war-making) produces a finer $P$-preserving object that factors uniquely through $\Phi$.
3. **Derivation vs.\ definition of necessity (Section 2.2, Section 3, Section 4):** Necessity is genuinely derived rather than true by definition. Definition 2.1 defines persistence without the six functions, Definition 2.2 defines each $\Impl_m$ through observable dispositions, Table 3 provides empirical falsifiers (requiring independent measurement of temptation shares and hazards to prevent post-hoc immunization), and Proposition 4.4(b) proves that each function can be absent in a persistent collective when its corresponding axiom fails. Section 3 also clearly separates the deductive necessity theorem from the empirical bridge hypothesis that human collectives satisfy the refined axioms.
4. **Dependency hypergraph and interdependence theorem (Section 5):** Using a directed hypergraph (Definitions 5.1 and 5.2) is mathematically well-motivated by Part III's finding that judicial determination requires legitimation *or* coercion rather than both, yielding the disjunctive hyperarc $(\{\pI,\pC\},\pJ)$. Proposition 5.10(c) and (e) show that this distinction is consequential: in $\Dstar \setminus \{\pC \to \pF\}$, the underlying digraph is strongly connected, yet the failure cascade $\cl(\{\pC\}) = \{\pC\}$ does not propagate unless either X5 adds $\pC \to \pF$ or the hyperarc is misspecified as two singleton arcs.
5. **Non-implication and conversion-dynamics theorems (Section 6, Section 8):** Lemma 6.5 and Theorem 6.6 cleanly separate Proposition A from Proposition B by realizing all four corners of $(U_t, \Integ_t) \in \{0,1\}^2$. Lemma 6.2 provides sharp star ($\le \bar c/3$) and clique ($\ge c k(k-1)/30$) bounds on $\Integ_t$. Theorem 8.5 rigorously establishes geometric decay of a coalition's foothold when $\rho(A)<1$, the $2/3$ asymptotic cap on $\Integ(S)_t$ when a single pillar has $Q_n=1$ and $\delta_n>0$, non-decay when $\delta_n=0$, and a uniform positive lower bound on $v^\top y_t$ via a quadratic Perron-Frobenius Lyapunov argument when $K$ is irreducible, $\max_n \delta_n < 1$, and $\rho(A) > 1$.
6. **Fairness of predecessor encodings and scope of novelty (Section 10):** Section 10.2 openly flags where encoding choices are contestable (whether $\pC$ belongs to goal attainment or integration in Parsons; whether $\pW$ belongs to inputs in Easton; placing administration with $\pC$ in Almond; collapsing communicative subsystems into $\pI$ in Luhmann), notes that Weber's typology is orthogonal and complementary to $\Phi$, and states in Remark 10.3 that failing to preserve $P$ is a difference of analytical purpose rather than a defect. Novelty is not overclaimed.
7. **Even-handedness across the six regime types (Section 9):** Section 9 and Table 4 treat all six regime types (market capitalism, communist party-states, social democracy, Islamic states, historical Catholic monarchies, and secular democracies) symmetrically as implementations of the same structure in $\mathcal{F}$, pairing each regime type's characteristic conversion path and capture episodes with its documented institutional checks and explicitly rejecting religious or ideological essentialism.
8. **Restraint of the theological section (Section 11):** Section 11 restricts itself to a disciplined typological reading grounded in mainstream apocalyptic scholarship (Bauckham, Yarbro Collins, Koester, Kraybill, Friesen, Price, Wink), explicitly repudiates futurist and historicist polemical identifications, and maps the imagery of Revelation 13 and 17 to 18 onto the captured corner $(1,1)$ of Proposition B rather than the universal structure of Proposition A.
9. **Clarity, structure, and LaTeX quality:** The architecture of the paper is clear, cross-references resolve properly, the TikZ diagram (Figure 1) accurately depicts $\Dstar$, and the tables are well-organized. Two minor bibliographic issues are noted below.

2. **Actor-set support in Witness Pair (a) of Proposition 7.5 (Section 7.3) and Proposition 10.1(b) (Section 10.2):**
   By Definition 7.2(i), $V_G$ is the set of actors who perform some practice in $G$. #### Minor Issues

1. **Wording of surjectivity for coarsenings (Section 7.3):**
   Section 7.3 defines a coarsening as *"a map $c:\Mset\to K\cup\{\bot\}$ onto a finite set $K$"*, and the first sentence of the proof of Proposition 7.5 writes *"A map from the six pillars onto $K\cup\{\bot\}$ that is not a bijection onto $K$"*.
