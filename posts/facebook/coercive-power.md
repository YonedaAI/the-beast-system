WHO ENFORCES THE RULES?

Wherever people live together, some will have reason to break the rules. Part V of our Beast System series asks what that forces.

The answer: violators have to end up worse off because of what they did. That requires some kind of sanction, but not a police force. Gossip, ostracism and coalitions against would-be bullies do the work among the Nuer, the Ju/'hoansi and in the commons Elinor Ostrom studied.

The paper then splits enforcement into two measurable margins. The conduct margin is how much likelier a rule-breaker is to be punished than a rule-follower. The allegiance margin is how much likelier an opponent is to be punished than a supporter. They move independently, so having enforcement does not mean it is being used for capture.

One consequence: when opponents are targeted regardless of conduct, the volume of coercion against them goes up, so budgets and arrest counts can mislead.

The ideology changes. The institutional functions remain.
