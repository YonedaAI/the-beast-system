Who produces what a governance system consumes? Part VI of the Beast System series treats production, payment and social reproduction as the sixth pillar of a framework in which six functions are present in every functioning polity.

From a maintenance axiom we prove that a persistent collective must produce at a positive average rate and must reproduce its producers through care labor, and that rents, tribute, migration or automation relocate production rather than remove it. The same dependence gives producers a latent veto: when outside revenue falls short of the institutions' maintenance requirement, a collective withdrawal of labor, taxes or trade threatens their persistence.

A bargaining model makes the channels exact. Producers' terms rise with their outside option and organized bargaining weight, fall linearly with the concentration of control over productive participation, and lose their withdrawal premium once rents cover the maintenance requirement. Restricting mobility pays only when the value of free exit exceeds the cost of enforcement, a condition that nests Domar's land-labor hypothesis and Brenner's emphasis on class organization. Every instrument that depresses producers' terms is held in another pillar, so the capture of production never originates inside it.

Six regime types are compared, and the function appears in all of them, with capture only under specific couplings. Revelation 13:16 and 17 are read typologically as the limiting case in which exchange is conditioned on allegiance. Nine hypotheses state what would count against the model.

The ideology changes. The institutional functions remain.
