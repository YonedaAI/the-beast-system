WHO INTERPRETS THE LAW?

No rule covers every case. At the edges, every rule can be read more than one way, and two parties who disagree cannot both be the judge.

Part III of our Beast System series shows that any group that lasts ends up settling nearly all such disputes through a procedure neither side controls. It does not need a courthouse. Leopard-skin chiefs, Barotse moots, Ju/'hoansi talking-out, qadi courts, the Parlement of Paris and modern constitutional courts all do the job.

Two twists. Where leaving is free, as among immediate-return foragers, adjudication stays thin. And when rules run as software, interpretation does not vanish. It moves to how inputs get classified.

A court is only as strong as its weakest link in a chain: disclosure, access, independence, and compliance by the powerful. Capture persists with a probability that depends on the product of those links, so one broken link can undo a court that looks good on paper.

The ideology changes. The institutional functions remain.
