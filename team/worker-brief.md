# Common brief for Parts I to VI (read fully before drafting)

Series: "The Six Pillars of the Beast System", seven Parts. Thesis: THE IDEOLOGY CHANGES; THE
INSTITUTIONAL FUNCTIONS REMAIN. Every governance system must answer six questions: who controls the
resources (F), who makes the laws (P), who interprets the laws (J), who shapes the beliefs (I),
who enforces the rules (C), who produces the wealth that sustains everything (W). Revelation 13
describes a system connecting authority, allegiance and economic participation; the comparative
framework is called the Hierarchy of the Beast System.

## Required paper spine (headings are sentence case, plain noun phrases)
1. Introduction: the guiding question; the universal-structure thesis; contributions.
2. The pillar as a layer of the multiplex network (shared notation; pillar-specific definitions you own).
3. Necessity of the pillar: derive it from its axiom (Lemma/Proposition with proof); show its minimal

5. Comparative analysis across ALL SIX regime types, equal depth (roughly equal length each):

   diversity); historical Catholic monarchies (Habsburg Spain, Bourbon France, Papal States);

10. Conclusion. References.
Formal content: Definitions, Lemmas/Propositions with real proofs where the claim is formal;
conditional comparative-static propositions stated as such. At least 20 pages compiled.

## Guardrails (hard)
- "Beast System" names the universal six-function structure present in every governance system. It
  is never a label for a religion, people, profession, regime, or named person.
- Equal analytical treatment of the six regimes. Islam and Catholicism appear as sources of political
  legitimacy in specific historical states, with internal diversity. No polemic, no ranking of faiths.
- Measurement before accusation: differentiation is not capture; public goods are a rival explanation.
- "Slavery" only in the source's disciplined, explicitly metaphorical sense.
