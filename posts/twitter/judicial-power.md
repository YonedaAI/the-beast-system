1/5 Who interprets the law? Part III of the Beast System series starts from a plain fact: every rule admits more than one reasonable reading at its edges, so rules cannot settle particular disputes alone.

2/5 Given costly self-help and costly exit, any persistent collective settles all but finitely many disputes through a procedure neither party controls. A function, not an office: leopard-skin chiefs, qadi courts, constitutional courts.

3/5 Two limits. When exit is costless the argument fails, matching the thin adjudication of immediate-return foragers. When rules run as code, interpretation moves to the classification of inputs instead of disappearing.

4/5 Contestability is a chain: disclosure, access, independence, compliance by the powerful. The chance a captured ruling persists depends on the product of these links, so an additive index can score a court well while one broken link leaves capture unchecked.

5/5 The ideology changes. The institutional functions remain.
