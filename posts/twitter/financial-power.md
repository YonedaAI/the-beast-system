1/5 Who controls the resources? Every governance system has to settle conflicting claims on finite goods. Part I of the Beast System series proves that a collective with no recognized allocation rule cannot persist, except with probability bounded away from zero.

2/5 In any collective that does persist, the share of conflicts settled by such a rule tends to one. The result is about a function, not an institution. In foraging bands the rule is a sharing norm, and control sits near the minimum concentration the index allows.

3/5 Necessity says nothing about concentration. A pillar can be needed and widely shared, or needed and held by a few. Aggregating subdomains also understates concentration by exactly the cross-domain variance of control shares.

4/5 The same function appears in market capitalism, communist party-states, social democracies, Islamic states, Catholic monarchies and secular democracies, held by different institutions. Each type shows both documented capture and documented checks.

5/5 The ideology changes. The institutional functions remain.
