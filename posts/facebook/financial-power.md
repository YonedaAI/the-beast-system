WHO CONTROLS THE RESOURCES?

Every society, from a foraging band to a modern state, has to settle who gets what when there is not enough to go around.

The first paper in our Beast System series proves something simple but easy to miss: a group with no recognized way of settling those claims cannot last. And in any group that does last, nearly all resource conflicts end up handled by some regular rule. In a foraging band that rule is a sharing norm. In a market economy it is property and contract.

Another result: needing the function does not tell you how concentrated it is. And if you average control across sectors, you understate concentration by exactly the variance of control shares between sectors.

The same function shows up in capitalist, communist party-state, social democratic, Islamic, historical Catholic monarchy and secular democratic systems, with documented abuses and documented checks in each.

The ideology changes. The institutional functions remain.
