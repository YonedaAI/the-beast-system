WHO MAKES THE RULES?

Any group whose members disagree but must still live under shared choices needs a way to decide. That is the political pillar in our Beast System series.

The paper shows that every lasting group has some rule-making function, though not any particular institution. Among stateless peoples it looks like camp discussion, lineage meetings, and splitting apart when talk fails.

Here is the surprising part: needing rule making says nothing about how concentrated it is. For a group of n members and any k from 1 to n, there is a decision procedure that meets the necessity condition with concentration of exactly 1/k. One decider or everyone, both can satisfy it.

Capture comes from elsewhere: monitoring that weakens as organizations grow, veto structures that protect old rules, and appointment channels that tie rule making to courts, security services and the economy.

The ideology changes. The institutional functions remain.
