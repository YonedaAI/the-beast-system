WHO SHAPES WHAT WE BELIEVE ABOUT THE RULES?

For people to cooperate, they need to know what the rules are, and know that everyone else knows. That is the ideological pillar in our Beast System series.

The paper shows every lasting group has some regular public practice that does this. In egalitarian societies it is talk, ridicule, ritual and kinship language, with no official in charge. The claim is about the function, not about any institution, and not about everyone believing the rules are right.

Then comes a result about endorsements. If an authority is captured, its endorsement tells you nothing. Independent authorities raise confidence in good rules and lower it in bad ones. And when several authorities are controlled together, an error is less likely to come to light than when the same authorities are independently controlled.

We look at academia, religion, media, schools and medical bodies across capitalist, communist party-state, social democratic, Islamic, historical Catholic monarchy and secular democratic systems.

The ideology changes. The institutional functions remain.
